ÄNDERN IN:

"To evaluate segment-level detection performance as deployed in real-time
operation, we applied the sliding window post-processing algorithm (see
Methods) to the raw network predictions and computed onset-based
segment-level precision, recall, and F1 with collar tolerances of 5, 10,
15, and 20 ms (Fig. 4C). A predicted segment was counted as a true
positive when its onset fell within ±collar ms of a ground-truth syllable
onset, regardless of offset timing. This onset-only criterion reflects the
primary requirement for closed-loop targeting, where reliable onset
detection determines feedback timing. Moove processes audio in frames of
64 samples at 44.1 kHz, yielding a temporal resolution of 1.45 ms per
frame; every frame receives an independent prediction, giving Moove a
defined and uniform onset-detection granularity. With a 10 ms collar,
onset-based F1 scores ranged from 0.917 to 0.986 across birds (Table 1),
with four birds achieving F1 ≥ 0.949. One bird (Bird 3, gy07bu07) showed
reduced performance (0.817) due to a mismatch between its tuned
classification threshold and the global sliding window parameters; when
evaluated with overlap inference, performance recovered to 0.917.
To benchmark segmentation performance, we compared Moove against an
energy-based segmentation baseline (evfuncs; Nicholson, 2021) using the
same algorithm that was employed to generate training labels, with the
amplitude threshold optimized per bird on the validation set (Fig. 4C,
dashed lines; Table 1). Unlike Moove, the energy-based baseline operates
on a smoothed amplitude envelope (smoothing window: 2 ms) and detects
onsets as threshold crossings of this envelope; it does not produce
frame-level predictions and does not use sliding window post-processing.
Moove outperformed the energy baseline in onset collar F1 at 10 ms for
all five birds (Bird 1: +0.06, Bird 2: +0.07, Bird 3: +0.004, Bird 4:
+0.07, Bird 5: +0.08).

Segmentation performance
We tested segmentation performance on five male Bengalese finches with different songs. Segmentation networks were trained separately for each bird to distinguish syllable segments from gaps in the audio stream. All network hyperparameters corresponded to the Moove default configuration. To assess robustness, we trained three replicates per bird with different random seeds for data splitting; all metrics are reported as mean ± standard deviation across replicates. Training data ranged from 108 to 579 manually corrected bouts across birds (mean: 235 ± 194 SD bouts), corresponding to 176–1407 s of audio, which were randomly split into training (70%), validation (15%), and test (15%) subsets (see Table 1 for individual bird data). Across all birds, training and validation loss curves converged smoothly (Fig. 4A), with early stopping terminating training after 10–24 epochs (mean: 15 ± 4 epochs). Framewise precision, recall, and F1-score were computed for each bird's test set (Fig. 4B), yielding mean F1 scores ranging from 0.882 to 0.962 across birds (Table 1). These results indicate that the network makes accurate predictions when distinguishing syllable segments from gaps and generalizes well across different individual birds.

To evaluate segment-level detection performance as deployed in real-time operation, we applied the sliding window post-processing algorithm (see Methods) to the raw network predictions and computed onset-based segment-level precision, recall, and F1 with collar tolerances of 5, 10, 15, and 20 ms (Fig. 4C). A predicted segment was counted as a true positive when its onset fell within ±collar ms of a ground-truth syllable onset, regardless of offset timing. This onset-only criterion reflects the primary requirement for closed-loop targeting, where reliable onset detection determines feedback timing. Moove processes audio in frames of 64 samples at 44.1 kHz, yielding a temporal resolution of 1.45 ms per frame; every frame receives an independent prediction, giving Moove a defined and uniform onset-detection granularity. With a 10 ms collar, onset-based F1 scores ranged from 0.817 to 0.986 across birds (Table 1). Four of five birds achieved F1 between 0.949 and 0.986; one bird (Bird 3) yielded 0.817 under standard parameters due to a mismatch between the bird-specific classification threshold and the global sliding window parameters, which caused many true onsets to be discarded. When using dense chunk sampling (step = 1 frame instead of the default step = hist_size), this bird achieved 0.917.
To benchmark segmentation performance, we compared Moove against an energy-based segmentation baseline (evfuncs; Nicholson, 2021) using the same algorithm that was employed to generate training labels, with the amplitude threshold optimized per bird on the validation set (Fig. 4C, dashed lines; Table 1). Unlike Moove, the energy-based baseline operates on a smoothed amplitude envelope (smoothing window: 2 ms) and detects onsets as threshold crossings of this envelope; it does not produce frame-level predictions and does not use sliding window post-processing. Moove outperformed the energy baseline in onset collar F1 at 10 ms for four of five birds (Bird 1: +0.06, Bird 2: +0.07, Bird 4: +0.07, Bird 5: +0.08). For Bird 3, the standard Moove result (0.817) fell below the baseline (0.913); using dense chunk sampling, Moove reached 0.917 (+0.004 vs. baseline).
To further quantify segmentation accuracy, we computed the signed onset time difference (predicted minus annotated) for all matched segments (true positives at 10 ms collar) across birds. Across all birds and replicates (n = 24,277 matched onsets), mean onset error was −0.5 ± 1.9 ms (median: −1.5 ms), confirming that detected onsets closely match the annotated boundaries. The median of −1.5 ms corresponds to approximately one audio chunk, consistent with the frame-level resolution of the network. The network's inference speed was approximately 0.73 ± 0.1 ms per audio chunk (batch size = 1) on a standard CPU, enabling real-time processing at the 44.1 kHz sampling rate.

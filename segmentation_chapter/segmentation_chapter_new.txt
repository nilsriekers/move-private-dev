Segmentation performance
We tested segmentation performance on five male Bengalese finches with different songs. Segmentation networks were trained separately for each bird to distinguish syllable segments from gaps in the audio stream. To assess robustness, we trained three replicates per bird with different random seeds for data splitting; all metrics are reported as mean ± standard deviation across replicates. Training data ranged from 108 to 579 manually verified bouts across birds (mean: 235 ± 194 SD bouts), corresponding to 176–1407 s of annotated audio, which were randomly split into training (70%), validation (15%), and test (15%) subsets (see Table 1 for individual bird data). Across all birds, training and validation loss curves converged smoothly (Fig. 4A), with early stopping terminating training after 10-24 epochs (mean: 15 ± 4 epochs). Framewise precision, recall, and F1-score were computed for each bird's test set (Fig. 4B), yielding mean F1 scores ranging from 0.882 to 0.962 across birds (Table 1). These results indicate that the network makes accurate predictions when distinguishing syllable segments from gaps and generalizes well across different individual birds.

To evaluate segment-level detection performance as deployed in real-time operation, we applied the sliding window post-processing algorithm (see Methods) to the raw network predictions and computed onset-based segment-level precision, recall, and F1 with collar tolerances of 5, 10, 15, and 20 ms (Fig. 4C). A predicted segment was counted as a true positive when its onset fell within ±collar ms of a ground-truth syllable onset, regardless of offset timing. This onset-only criterion reflects the primary requirement for closed-loop targeting, where reliable onset detection determines feedback timing. Moove processes audio in frames of 64 samples at 44.1 kHz, yielding a temporal resolution of 1.45 ms per frame; every frame receives an independent prediction, giving Moove a defined and uniform onset-detection granularity. With a 10 ms collar, onset-based F1 scores ranged from 0.817 to 0.986 across birds (Table 1), with four birds achieving F1 ≥ 0.949. One bird (Bird 3) achieved 0.817, which is attributable to a mismatch between the bird-specific classification threshold (0.95) and the global sliding window parameters: the sliding window requires 3 out of 5 consecutive frames to be classified as syllable to confirm an onset, but the high threshold produces sparse frame-level predictions, causing many true onsets to be discarded. When evaluated without sliding window post-processing, Bird 3 achieved an onset collar F1 of 0.926, confirming that the network itself detects syllable onsets accurately and that the sliding window parameters should be tuned jointly with the threshold.
To benchmark segmentation performance, we compared Moove against an energy-based segmentation baseline (evfuncs; Nicholson, 2021) using the same algorithm that was employed to generate training labels, with the amplitude threshold optimized per bird on the validation set (Fig. 4C, dashed lines; Table 1). Unlike Moove, the energy-based baseline operates on a smoothed amplitude envelope (smoothing window: 2 ms) and detects onsets as threshold crossings of this envelope; it does not produce frame-level predictions and does not use sliding window post-processing. Moove outperformed the energy baseline in onset collar F1 at 10 ms for four of five birds (Bird 1: +0.06, Bird 2: +0.07, Bird 4: +0.07, Bird 5: +0.08). Bird 3 is the sole exception (Moove +SW: 0.817 vs. Baseline: 0.913); however, Moove without sliding window post-processing achieves 0.926 for Bird 3, exceeding the baseline, confirming that the raw network discriminates syllable onsets accurately and that the performance deficit is attributable to the sliding window parameter mismatch described above.
To further quantify segmentation accuracy, we computed the signed onset time difference (predicted minus annotated) for all matched segments (true positives at 10 ms collar) across birds. Across all birds and replicates (n = 24,277 matched onsets), mean onset error was −0.5 ± 1.9 ms (median: −1.5 ms), confirming that detected onsets closely match manual annotations. The median of −1.5 ms corresponds to approximately one audio chunk (1.45 ms at 44.1 kHz), consistent with the frame-level resolution of the network output. The network's inference speed was approximately 0.73 ms ± 0.1 ms per audio chunk (batch size = 1) on a standard CPU, enabling real-time processing given that new 64-sample chunks arrive every 1.45 ms at the 44.1 kHz sampling rate.
